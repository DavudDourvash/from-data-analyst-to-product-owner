# Week 01 — Product Thinking & Problem Discovery

## What I Studied

* Read the Preface to the Second Edition of *INSPIRED* by Marty Cagan.
* Read Chapters 1–6.
* Focused on understanding:

  * What a Product Manager / Product Owner is responsible for
  * Product teams and their role
  * Product vs. project thinking
  * Problem vs. solution
  * Output vs. outcome
  * Why traditional, stakeholder-driven product processes often fail
  * The importance of validating ideas before investing heavily in building them

## What I Learned

One of the most important lessons for me was that product work should not start with a solution.

For example, when someone says:

> "We need a Heatmap."

My first reaction should not be to define the feature or write a user story. I should first understand:

* What problem are we trying to solve?
* Who has this problem?
* How is the problem currently being solved?
* How important is the problem?
* What evidence do we have?
* What outcome are we trying to achieve?

I also started to distinguish between **Output** and **Outcome**.

For example:

* Output: Build a dashboard.
* Outcome: Reduce the time an analyst needs to turn available data into a useful insight.

This changed the way I look at the Analytics Platform I am working on.

## Applying Product Thinking to My Product

Initially, I described the problem mainly as:

> Analysts do not have direct access to raw data.

After the exercises, I realized that this may be only part of the problem.

A deeper hypothesis is:

> Analysts may spend significant time waiting for data and preparing data for analysis, leaving less time for the actual analysis and insight generation.

This is still a **problem hypothesis**, not a validated product requirement.

## Discovery Exercise

I practiced asking follow-up questions about an analyst who could not complete an analysis by the deadline.

The conversation revealed several potential sources of delay:

1. Waiting for data to become available.
2. Preparing the data model.
3. Building visualizations and dashboards.

This helped me understand that the first problem I identify may not necessarily be the root problem.

I also learned that when a user proposes a solution such as:

> "I need a pre-built data model."

or

> "I need a powerful dashboard builder."

I should not immediately turn those statements into product requirements.

Instead, I should ask **why** the user needs them and investigate the underlying problem.

## What I Still Don't Understand

* The exact distinction between Product Manager and Product Owner.
* How to determine which problem should be prioritized when different users have different problems.
* How to distinguish a high-impact problem from a problem that is simply mentioned frequently.
* How to validate whether the problem hypothesis for the Analytics Platform is actually true.

## Key Takeaway

The most important shift in my thinking this week:

> **Don't start with "What should we build?" Start with "What problem are we trying to solve, for whom, and what outcome do we want?"**

## Next Step

Continue learning Product Discovery and practice identifying and validating user problems before thinking about solutions.
